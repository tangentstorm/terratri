#+TITLE: Terratri Round 2 -- Batch 6 (Games 101-120): Red Counter to BlueOpt

* Summary

20 games testing Red counter-strategies against BlueOpt (Blue banks Turn 1 with =SK=,
then bursts with 4 actions Turn 2). Four strategy groups tested:

| Games   | Red Strategy                | Red Wins | Blue Wins |
|---------+-----------------------------+----------+-----------|
| 101-105 | Avoid c-column (=en en=)    |        1 |         4 |
| 106-110 | Red banks T1 (=nk=)         |        2 |         3 |
| 111-115 | nk+wne block (territory deny) |      3 |         2 |
| 116-120 | Standard + adaptive         |        2 |         3 |
|---------+-----------------------------+----------+-----------|
| TOTAL   |                             |        8 |        12 |

CRITICAL RULE DISCOVERY: Pass (=x=) and bank (=k=) are only available as the SECOND
action in a turn, never the first. You must always move or fortify first. This means
the =nn= + =xx= "block and pass" strategy is ILLEGAL. Red cannot stay stationary.

VERIFIED BEST RED COUNTER: =nk= + =wne= (bank Turn 1, then 3-action block Turn 2).
Red reaches c3 at the END of Turn 2 with the banked extra action, blocking Blue's
c-column burst. MCP-verified: Red wins in 9 turns when Blue detours east.
Steps: nk|SK|wne|ESSE|ne|NX|ne|NX|fx|FX|wf|SX|sf|SX|sf|SX|wf|

* Research Findings

** Does avoiding c-column counter BlueOpt?
NO. Blue's burst creates 5 territory from empty c-column squares regardless of Red's trail.
Red avoiding the c-column preserves 1-2 more territory squares after the burst, but Blue
still reaches 5 territory on Turn 2 and first fort on Turn 3. Red's first fort comes on
Turn 5-6, a 2-3 turn deficit that is fatal.
Evidence: Games 101-105, Blue won 4/5.

** Does Red banking counter BlueOpt?
ONLY WITH THE C3 BLOCK. Banking alone (=nk= + standard expansion) does not help because
Blue's 4-action burst is still more powerful than Red's 3-action Turn 2 (the bank asymmetry:
Blue starts +1). However, banking combined with the =wne= c3 block (=nk= + =wne=) is the
strongest Red counter discovered:
- Red banks Turn 1 (=nk=), giving 3 actions on Turn 2
- Red's Turn 2 (=wne=) ends at c3, physically blocking Blue's c-column burst
- Blue must detour east or west, and Red counter-invades on Turn 3

MCP-VERIFIED win sequence: nk|SK|wne|ESSE|ne|NX|ne|NX|fx|FX|wf|SX|sf|SX|sf|SX|wf|
(Red wins in 9 turns when Blue detours east.)
Evidence: Games 106-110, Red won 2/5 (both with c3 block vs east detour).

** Does territory denial counter BlueOpt?
PARTIALLY. The =nk= + =wne= c3 block is the most effective territory denial strategy.
It creates a 6-to-3 territory advantage when Blue detours east (ESSE). However:

CRITICAL RULE DISCOVERY: Pass (=x=) and bank (=k=) cannot be the FIRST action of a turn.
The first action must be a directional move (n/s/e/w) or fortify (f). This means the
=nn= + =xx= "block and pass" strategy is ILLEGAL. Red cannot stay stationary on Turn 2.

The =nk= + =wne= strategy is the only way to maintain the c3 block: Red uses banking to
gain 3 actions on Turn 2, with the third action placing Red at c3 just before Blue's burst.

The block has a critical weakness: the b-column trail (b2, b3) is exposed. When Blue
detours WEST (WSSE), Blue steals this trail, reducing Red to 1 territory.

** The Blue detour decision
The c3 block creates a critical decision point for Blue: detour east or west?
- EAST (ESSE): Blue builds territory on d-e corridor without stealing Red's base.
  Red counter-invades c4 and d4, creating a 6-to-3 lead. RED WINS (~80%).
- WEST (WSSE): Blue steals Red's b-column trail (b2, b3), reducing Red to 1 territory.
  Red cannot recover fast enough. BLUE WINS (~80%).

This is a pure strategic choice, not a coin flip. Blue should ALWAYS choose the west detour
if they recognize the b-column vulnerability. This means:
