#+TITLE: Terratri Strategy Guide
#+SUBTITLE: Synthesized from 100 AI Self-Play Games

* Overview

This guide distills strategic insights from 100 games of Terratri played by AI
agents using various strategies. The tournament covered opening theory, banking
tactics, fortification timing, spatial patterns, and endgame technique.

* The Fundamentals

** Win Condition

Place all 5 forts on the board. This is a race -- there is no territorial
victory condition. Territory only matters because you need 5+ squares to
fortify, and you need fort supply (5 minus forts on board minus banked moves).

** Turn Structure

Each turn gives you 2 base actions. Options for each action:
- Move in a cardinal direction (n/s/e/w)
- Fortify your current square (f) -- requires 5+ territory and fort supply > 0
- Pass/end turn (x) -- available as 2nd action
- Bank a move (k) -- available as 2nd action, stores a move for later

Banked moves let you take extra actions on future turns (3, 4, 5+ action turns).

** The Territory Threshold

The single most important number in Terratri is *5*. You cannot place forts
until you control 5 territory squares. Every strategic decision should be
evaluated against this threshold:
- Am I moving toward 5 territory?
- Will this move cost me territory (dropping below 5)?
- Can I deny my opponent 5 territory?

* Opening Theory

** Red Openings (Ranked)

Red moves first from c1 (center bottom). The opening determines how quickly
Red reaches 5 territory.

*** Tier 1: North-first openings (7-turn wins in symmetric play)
- =ne= (north-east): c1->c2->d2. Center-east development. Clean and efficient.
- =nw= (north-west): c1->c2->b2. Contests Blue's western expansion early.
- =en= (east-north): c1->d1->d2. Develops the east flank smoothly.

These reach 5-6 territory by Turn 3, enabling fortification from Turn 4.

*** Tier 2: Flanking openings (8-turn wins, viable)
- =wn= (west-north): c1->b1->b2. Leaves east side undeveloped. Blue can exploit.
- =nn= (north-north): c1->c2->c3. Fast center control but narrow territory.
- =ee= (east-east): c1->d1->e1. Edge play, efficient for column fortification.
- =ww= (west-west): c1->b1->a1. Edge play on the west side.

*** Tier 3: Avoid
- =ew= (east-west): c1->d1->c1. Returns to start. Wastes entire first turn.
- Any opening that returns to a previously visited square.

** Blue Openings

Blue starts at c5 (center top) with 1 banked move, giving 3 actions on Turn 1.

*** Standard Expansion
- =SES= (south-east-south): c5->c4->d4->d3. Strong center-east presence.
- =SSE= (south-south-east): c5->c4->c3->d3. Deep center penetration.
- =WSW/WSE= (west-south-west/east): Flanking approaches.

*** BlueOpt (strongest Blue opening)
- =SK= (south-bank): c5->c4, bank. Only 1 territory but saves 2 banked moves total.
- Then Turn 2: =SSES= burst through Red's trail for 5 territory in one turn.
- This is the strongest Blue opening found across 100 games.

** Opening Principles

1. *Move toward the center first* -- north-component openings are strongest for Red.
2. *Claim unique territory* -- never revisit squares you've already marked.
3. *Reach 5 territory by Turn 3* -- this enables fortification from Turn 4.
4. *Blue should consider banking Turn 1* -- the BlueOpt SK opening is powerful.

* Fortification Strategy

** When to Fortify

Fortify as soon as you have 5+ territory and fort supply > 0. Every turn you
delay fortification, your opponent can place a fort and pull ahead in the race.

#+begin_quote
"Territory without forts is ephemeral." -- Game 18 (Batch 1)
#+end_quote

In Games 5 and 18, the "late fort" strategy (maximize territory before fortifying)
consistently lost to "early fort" (fortify immediately at 5 territory). Red had 8
territory in Game 18 but zero forts, and lost to Blue's 5 territory + 2 forts.

** The Fort-Move Pattern

The optimal endgame technique is alternating fortify-move:
- =sf= (south, fortify), =nf= (north, fortify), =ef= (east, fortify)
- Or =fs= (fortify, south), =fn= (fortify, north), etc.

This places one fort per turn while positioning for the next. Games 56 and 60
demonstrate that when both players use this pattern, Red wins in 8 turns purely
from first-mover advantage.

** Fort Placement Principles

*** Connected forts are stronger than scattered forts
Fort walls (lines of adjacent forts) serve dual purpose:
- Score toward the 5-fort win condition
- Block enemy pawn movement

Game 11 (Batch 1) showed Blue building a wall from c4 to a2 that Red couldn't
breach. Games 61-63 (Batch 4) showed Red's row-5 fort sweep as devastating.

*** Fort on enemy territory creates double advantage
When you fortify on a square the opponent previously claimed, you simultaneously:
- Gain a fort (progressing toward win)
- Deny opponent a territory square (potentially dropping them below 5)

Games 62, 73, 75, and 78 (Batch 4) demonstrated this -- Red's eastward fort sweep
through Blue's territory was often game-winning.

*** Blocking forts disrupt opponent plans
Game 75 (Batch 4) showed the key counter to Blue's double-fort: Red places a
fort at c4, directly blocking Blue's northward fort column. This forced Blue to
detour, costing a critical tempo.

* Banking Strategy

** Banking Overview

Banking (k/K) stores an action for later use. Each banked move costs 1 fort
supply. Blue starts with 1 free banked move.

| Banked | Turn Actions | Fort Supply Cost |
|--------+--------------+------------------|
|      0 |            2 |                0 |
|      1 |            3 |                1 |
|      2 |            4 |                2 |
|      3 |            5 |                3 |
|      4 |            6 |                4 |

** The Banking Spectrum

*** No banking (pure expansion)
Consistent, reliable. Fort every turn once at 5 territory.
Win rate: ~50% (depends on strategy).

*** Light banking (1-2 banks)
Bank 1-2 moves for a tactical burst. The optimal balance.
Enables 3-4 action turns for catching up or making a push.

*** Heavy banking (3-4 banks)
High-risk, high-reward. The "bank-4-burst-3-forts" pattern (Games 49, 59 in Batch 3)
places 3 forts in one massive turn. But banking 4+ turns gives the opponent free
fortification time.

*** Maximum banking (5 banks)
5 banked = 0 fort supply = cannot place any forts until you spend banked moves.
Games 3 and 9 (Batch 1) showed 7-action turns that are spectacular but usually too late.

** Optimal Banking Tactics

*** Red: Bank-2-Burst
- Turns 1-2: Move + bank each turn (=nk=, =wk=)
- Turn 3: 4-action burst: move-move-move-fortify
- Then: Normal fort-move pattern

This reaches the same position as normal play but is neither better nor worse
(Game 86, Batch 5). Banking as Red is a style choice, not an advantage.

*** Blue: BlueOpt (Bank-then-Burst)
- Turn 1: =SK= (move south, bank). Total: 2 banked moves.
- Turn 2: =SSES= (4-action burst through Red's unfortified territory trail).
- Turn 3+: Fort aggressively from the bottom of the board.

This is the *strongest Blue strategy discovered*. It exploits the fact that Red's
early territory is unfortified and can be overwritten. Games 92, 96, and 97 (Batch 5)
show it beating Smart Red consistently.

*** Blue: Double-Fort (FEF/FWF)
- Turns 1-3: Preserve banked move while expanding (use =X= to end turn early)
- Turn 4: =FEF= (fortify, east, fortify) or =FWF= (fortify, west, fortify)
- This places 2 forts in one turn, compensating for Blue's second-mover disadvantage.

Games 66-70 (Batch 4) systematically proved this technique:
- FEF wins in 7 turns when Red doesn't counter
- FWF is the mirror variant (works identically)
- Red's counter: block the fort direction with a fort at c4 (Game 75, 80)

** Banking Traps

1. *Banking too long*: Banking 4+ turns as Red gives Blue 3+ free forts (Games 3, 21).
2. *Fort supply exhaustion*: Banked moves reduce fort supply. 5 banked = 0 supply.
3. *No opponent pressure*: Banking only works if the opponent doesn't build an
   insurmountable fort lead during the banking phase.

* Territory Control

** Spatial Patterns

*** Edge/Column play
Moving along an edge (a-column, e-column, row 1, row 5) creates linear territory
that is easy to fortify. Games 88 and 94 (Batch 5) showed Wall and Edge strategies
winning by building efficient fort columns.

Advantage: Simple, efficient path. No decision-making overhead.
Disadvantage: Opponent can claim the entire center uncontested.

*** Center play
Center control (c3 area) is positionally strong but has a fatal flaw: the center
only has 4-5 reachable squares without doubling back. Game 90 (Batch 5) showed the
"Center" strategy trapped in infinite oscillation, never reaching 5 territory.

Advantage: Central position reaches all board quadrants.
Disadvantage: Oscillation trap. Smaller territory ceiling.

*** Diagonal play
Moving in alternating north-east / south-west patterns covers ground efficiently.
Game 17 (Batch 1) showed diagonal Red reaching all four corners before Blue's
defensive forts could block.

Advantage: Wide coverage, good fort placement variety.
Disadvantage: Can create gaps in territory that opponents exploit.

** Territory Denial

When you move onto an opponent's territory square, you claim it. This is a
powerful tactic in the mid-game:

1. *Reduces opponent's territory count* -- potentially below 5 (blocking forts)
2. *Increases your territory count* -- potentially reaching 5 (enabling forts)
3. *Disrupts opponent's fort path* -- forces them to rebuild before fortifying

Game 78 (Batch 4): Blue's aggressive T1 bank-spend (SES toward Red) backfired --
Red captured Blue's territory and invaded southward, leaving Blue permanently below
the 5-territory threshold.

* Counter-Strategies

** Countering Aggressive Play

Aggression (rushing toward the opponent) wins when it disrupts their territory.
It loses when the opponent simply avoids it.

Counter: *Flanking*. Move away from the aggressor and build territory on the
opposite side of the board. Games 41-45 (Batch 3) showed counter-strategies
winning 5/5 against aggressive Red.

** Countering Banking

Against a banking player, you have several free turns to expand and fortify.

Counter: *Steady fortification*. Place one fort per turn while the opponent banks.
By the time they burst, you'll have 3+ forts to their 0. Games 21-22 (Batch 2)
showed that banking rarely overcomes a 3+ fort deficit.

Exception: If the burstor reaches 5 territory and can place 3 forts in one turn
(bank-4-burst pattern), the burst can overcome a 2-fort deficit (Game 25).

** Countering the Double-Fort (FEF)

Blue's double-fort at Turn 4 is a tempo play. Red's counter:

1. *Read the direction*: FEF goes east, FWF goes west.
2. *Block the path*: Place a fort in Blue's planned column (c4 blocks FEF-northward).
3. *Capture their territory*: Fort into Blue's territory to drop their count below 5.

Games 75 and 80 (Batch 4) showed this counter working. Red wins 8 turns by blocking
at c4 then sweeping east through Blue's territory.

** Countering BlueOpt

BlueOpt (bank Turn 1, burst Turn 2 through Red's trail) is harder to counter.
It beats both Smart and Rush Red strategies (Games 92, 96, 97 in Batch 5).

Potential counters (not fully proven):
- Red avoids the c-column entirely (e.g., =en= to d1->d2 instead of =nn= through c2)
- Red plays BlueOpt-as-Red (Game 98), banking to out-tempo Blue
- The definitive Red counter to BlueOpt remains an open problem

* Strategy Tier List

Based on 100 games of tournament data:

** S Tier
- *BlueOpt*: Bank T1 then burst through opponent's trail. Won 5/5 games played.
  The strongest Blue strategy and strong as Red too.

** A Tier
- *Smart/Fort-focused*: Expand efficiently, fortify immediately at 5 territory,
  fort-move pattern. The reliable workhorse. Red wins 7-8 turns in most matchups.
- *Double-fort (FEF)*: Blue's key technique. Compensates for second-mover
  disadvantage. Beatable but forces Red to read and counter.

** B Tier
- *Edge/Wall*: Viable, efficient fort paths along board edges. Slightly less
  flexible than center approaches but reliable.
- *Banking burst*: High-risk, high-reward. Bank-4-burst-3-forts can overcome
  deficits but dangerous if opponent fortifies aggressively during banking phase.

** C Tier
- *Aggressive/Raider*: Disrupts opponent but can waste tempo chasing.
  Works against passive opponents, fails against flankers.
- *Expansion*: Pure territory maximization. Equivalent to Smart in practice
  but sometimes fortifies too late.

** D Tier
- *Defensive*: Cedes initiative. "Defense loses in Terratri" -- territory
  control requires active expansion.
- *Late fort*: Maximizing territory before fortifying. Creates longest games
  (19 turns) but gives opponent free forts.

** F Tier
- *Center oscillation*: Greedy center-seeking without memory. Trapped in
  infinite loops, never reaches 5 territory. Catastrophically bad.
- *Random*: Wastes actions on passes and aimless wandering. Still wins as
  Red (!) due to first-mover advantage, showing how strong the tempo is.

* Key Principles Summary

1. *Reach 5 territory by Turn 3*. This is the single most important goal.
2. *Fortify immediately at threshold*. One delayed fort can cost the game.
3. *Fort-move-fort-move*. The optimal endgame pattern. Every turn should place a fort.
4. *Connected forts > scattered forts*. Fort walls block movement and score simultaneously.
5. *Territory without forts is ephemeral*. Unfortified territory can be overwritten.
6. *Banking is timing*. 1-2 banks for tactical advantage; 4+ banks for desperate comeback.
7. *Blue's banked move is key*. Used for double-fort (FEF) or BlueOpt burst, it compensates for going second.
8. *Red's advantage is tempo*. In symmetric play, Red wins by one turn. Blue must break symmetry.
9. *Read your opponent*. Counter aggression with flanking. Counter banking with steady forts.
10. *Never oscillate*. Always move to new/unclaimed squares. Revisiting wastes tempo.

* Appendix: The Terratri Metagame

The metagame has a rock-paper-scissors quality:

- *Steady fort-play* beats banking (free forts while opponent saves)
- *Banking burst* beats slow expansion (burst overcomes territory lead)
- *Counter-flanking* beats aggression (avoid confrontation, build elsewhere)
- *Aggression* beats defense (claim contested territory, deny forts)
- *BlueOpt* beats standard play (exploits unfortified trail weakness)
- *Blocking forts* beat double-fort (read direction, block column)

No single strategy dominates all others, though BlueOpt comes closest for Blue
and Smart/Fort-focused is the safest for Red.

The deepest strategic question remains open: what is Red's optimal counter to
BlueOpt? The 100-game tournament suggests Red should either avoid the c-column
(denying Blue the burst path) or adopt the BlueOpt banking pattern as Red
(leveraging first-mover advantage with the burst).
