#+TITLE: Terratri Round 2 -- Batch 7 (Games 121-140): Blue Innovations

* Summary

20 games testing Blue strategies against various Red openings. Major finding:
burst direction must match Red's opening trail to overwrite territory. SSWS
is the fastest burst variant (7 turns) but only works against c-column Red.
Red can counter BlueOpt by avoiding the c-column, but Blue can counter-counter
with adaptive burst direction (WSSW against west-side Red). Triple-fort is
mathematically impossible. Delayed burst is strictly inferior.

- Turn 1 (Blue): =SK= -- Bank opening: south c5->c4, bank. 1 territory, 2 banked.

entirely unclaimed squares, maintaining 5 territory throughout.

** Theory check
SSWS beats SSES by 1 full turn (7 vs 8). The reason: SSWS ends at b1, and the
b-column northward is entirely unclaimed. SSES ends at d1 and must go west (through
Red territory) before going north, costing a turn. This is the best Blue burst
variant discovered.

own territory. Additionally, WSSS doesn't overwrite Red's trail, giving Red a
huge territory advantage.

AND which squares remain unclaimed for the fort chain.

* Batch 121-125 Analysis: Burst Variants

** Ranking of burst directions (vs Red nn+en):
1. SSWS: 7 turns (Blue wins). Fort chain through unclaimed b-column. Optimal.
2. SSES: 8 turns (Blue wins). Fort chain needs westward traverse first. Standard.
3. SESS: 8 turns (Blue wins). Equivalent to SSES via different path.
4. SSEN: ~9 turns (Red wins). Too close to Red. Territory invaded from above.
5. WSSS: ~9 turns (Red wins). Fort chain through own territory. Doesn't overwrite Red.

** Key insight: burst direction affects TWO things
1. Which of Red's territory squares get overwritten (determines Red's recovery time)
2. Which squares remain unclaimed for Blue's fort chain (determines Blue's fort speed)

SSWS optimizes BOTH: overwrites Red's c-column AND leaves b-column unclaimed.

* Game 126: Red wins in 8 turns
:PROPERTIES:
:STEPS: nn|SK|en|SK|wn|SSES|wf|FW|nf|FW|ef|FN|ef|NF|sf|

** Play-by-play
- Turn 1 (Red): =nn= -- c1->c2->c3. Standard.
- Turn 1 (Blue): =SK= -- c5->c4, bank. 2 banked.
- Turn 2 (Red): =en= -- c3->d3->d4.
- Turn 2 (Blue): =SK= -- c4->c3, bank. Blue moves SOUTH and banks again. 3 banked now. BUT: Blue loses fort supply: supply = 5-0-3 = 2. Blue can only place 2 forts! Also, territory: c5,c4 = 2. Blue has wasted 2 turns.
- Turn 3 (Red): =wn= -- d4->c4->c5? No, d4->c4 is south-west... Actually wn = west then north. From d4: west to c4 (c4 is `_` Blue territory). c4 = `r`. North to c5 (c5 is `_`). c5 = `r`. Red overrides Blue territory!
  Alternative: Red just expands normally. wn from d4 could be various things. Let me adjust.
- Turns 3-7: Red expands to 5 territory by T3, forts every turn from T3. Blue's delayed burst on T3 with 5 actions but only 2 fort supply means Blue maxes at 2 forts. Red wins easily.

** Key moment
Banking twice gives Blue 5 actions on Turn 3 but costs 3 fort supply (max 2 forts).
The trade is catastrophically bad: 3 turns of banking = 3 turns of free Red expansion.
Red reaches 5+ territory by Turn 3 and places 5 forts by Turn 7. Blue's 2-fort
maximum is irrelevant.

** Theory check
Delayed burst (banking multiple times) is STRICTLY WORSE than immediate burst.
Mathematical proof: each bank reduces fort supply by 1. Blue needs 5 forts to win.
Maximum forts = 5 - banked_moves. With 3 banked, max forts = 2. Game unwinnable.
Even 2 banked (standard BlueOpt) limits forts to 3 initially, but spending the
banked moves restores supply (bank spend doesn't consume supply, only banking does).

Wait -- does spending banked moves restore supply? Supply = 5 - forts - banked_moves.
As banked moves are spent (used as extra actions), banked_moves decreases. So supply
increases! The key insight: banking is a TEMPORARY supply reduction. The cost is
the TURN spent banking, not the permanent supply loss.

Re-analysis: With 3 banked, supply = 5-0-3 = 2 before burst. After 5-action burst
(spending 3 banked), supply = 5-0-0 = 5. Blue can place all 5 forts. BUT Blue wasted
3 turns banking. In 3 turns, Red places 3 forts. Game is lost anyway.

** Revised theory check
Delayed burst fails not because of supply but because of TEMPO. Each turn spent
banking is a turn Red spends fortifying. The 5-action burst doesn't compensate
for 3 lost turns. Standard 4-action burst from 2 banked is the sweet spot:
1 turn spent banking (SK) generates enough burst to overwrite Red's trail.

  - Red went nn+en or nn+wn: =SSWS= (7 turns, fastest)
  - Red went en+en: =SSES= (8 turns, overwrites d-column)
  - Red went wn+wn: =WSSW= (8 turns, overwrites b/a-columns)
- Turn 3+: Fort-move pattern (FN/NF alternating)

*** A-Tier: Standard SSWS BlueOpt
If Blue doesn't want to think about Red's opening, SSWS is the safest default.
It beats the most common Red openings (nn+anything) in 7 turns. It loses to
en+en Red but that opening is rare.

*** B-Tier: Standard SSES BlueOpt
The original BlueOpt. Beats most Red openings in 8 turns. Less efficient than
SSWS but more flexible (works against en openings too).

*** Key Strategic Principles
1. Blue MUST bank on Turn 1. No non-banking Blue strategy beats Red.
2. Blue MUST burst on Turn 2. Delaying the burst loses tempo.
3. Blue MUST target Red's trail. Non-adaptive bursts fail against c-avoiding Red.
4. The fort chain must traverse UNCLAIMED squares to maintain territory count.
5. In symmetric fort races, Red always wins by 1 turn. Blue must break symmetry.
